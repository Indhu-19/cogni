# Budgeting Principles

## The 50/30/20 Rule
The 50/30/20 rule is a simple budgeting framework:
- **50%** of after-tax income on **needs** (rent, groceries, utilities, transport, minimum debt payments).
- **30%** on **wants** (dining out, entertainment, subscriptions, hobbies).
- **20%** on **savings and debt repayment** (emergency fund, investments, extra loan payments).

It is flexible: adjust percentages if you live in a high-cost city or have aggressive debt goals.

## Emergency Fund
An emergency fund covers unexpected expenses (job loss, medical bills, major repairs).
Target: **3–6 months** of essential living expenses in a liquid account (savings or money-market).
Build it before heavy investing if you have no buffer.

## Avalanche vs Snowball Debt Methods
**Debt Avalanche**: Pay minimums on all debts, put extra money toward the **highest interest rate** debt first. Mathematically optimal (least interest paid).

**Debt Snowball**: Pay minimums on all debts, put extra money toward the **smallest balance** first. Psychological wins from quick payoffs can improve motivation.

Choose avalanche for pure cost minimization; snowball if you need momentum.

## Pay Yourself First
Treat savings as a non-negotiable expense. Automate transfers to savings/investments right after payday, then budget the rest. This prevents lifestyle creep from consuming all income.

## Zero-Based Budgeting
Every rupee of income is assigned a job (bills, savings, spending categories) until income − allocations = 0. Forces intentional spending and reveals leaks.

## Tracking and Review
- Track expenses for at least 1–2 months to see real patterns.
- Review the budget monthly and adjust categories.
- Separate needs from wants honestly.
- Use categories that match how you actually spend (food delivery, subscriptions, transport, etc.).

## Key Habits
1. Spend less than you earn.
2. Build an emergency fund.
3. Avoid high-interest consumer debt.
4. Invest consistently for long-term goals.
5. Review and adjust regularly.
